# Custom Image Prompt — WWE PPV (Built from Reference Template)

This prompt was built by starting with an existing template, swapping the reference image, and customizing each section line by line. No text overlay. No brand style block.

---

## Final Prompt (copy and paste)

```
Use the provided reference image for the model's facial features, body proportions, hair, and overall appearance.

The model is in the middle of the ring holding the WWE United States Championship belt on his shoulder.

His expression is angry and excited.

One of his arms is raised with a fist in the air.

He is wearing a black Von Dutch hat with a white logo, diamond earrings, a Cuban link chain, a red hoodie with a black N.W.O. logo, distressed blue jeans, and red sneakers.

Setting:
A PPV event live at Madison Square Garden with a sold-out crowd. The environment feels authentic, modern, and performance-driven.

Lighting:
Well-lit environment on the wrestling ring.

Camera & Composition:
Aspect ratio: 16:9
Shot type: Full-body portrait, wide shot
Camera position: Elevated at the top of the arena, zooming in on the ring
Angle: Slight three-quarter angle on the model; lens points slightly upward to emphasize dominance
Distance to subject: Approximately 40–60 feet from the model
Framing: Model positioned on the right side of the frame; crowd and arena screens fill the background; large negative space on the left side of the frame
Horizon: Slightly tilted upward for a grand, heroic perspective
Depth of field: Shallow — sharp focus on face and upper body; background crowd slightly softened
Lens: 24–35mm full-frame equivalent
```

## How the Camera Block Was Built

1. Start with basics: `16:9`, full-body, three-quarter angle.
2. Find a reference image (e.g. a classic WWE arena shot on Google Images).
3. Ask the AI: *"Analyze this image — give me the angle and camera settings."*
4. Paste the analysis into your prompt and adapt it to your scene.

Reference analysis fields to pull from any shot:
- Camera angle (low, high, eye-level)
- Camera height and position
- Distance to subject
- Framing (wide, medium, close-up; subject placement)
- Horizon tilt
- Lens estimate
- Depth of field
