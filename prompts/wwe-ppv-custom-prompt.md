## Final Prompt (copy and paste)

Attach your reference photo, then paste this:

```
Use the provided reference image for the model's facial features, body proportions, hair, and overall appearance.

The model is in the middle of the ring holding the WWE United States Championship belt on his shoulder.

His expression is angry and excited.

One of his arms is raised with a fist in the air.

He is wearing a black Von Dutch hat with a white logo, diamond earrings, a Cuban link chain, a red hoodie with a black N.W.O. logo, distressed blue jeans, and red sneakers.

Setting:
A PPV event live at Madison Square Garden with a sold-out crowd. The environment feels authentic, modern, and performance-driven.

Lighting:
Well-lit environment on the wrestling ring. Practical arena lighting — multiple high-powered spotlights, LED screens, crowd lighting, and stage effects. Strong back and rim lights with cool blue and white beams. Bright fill from LED displays.

Camera & Composition:
Aspect ratio: 16:9
Shot type: Full-body portrait, wide shot
Camera angle: Low-angle shot
Camera position: Slightly below waist height, like from ringside or just behind the barricade; lens points slightly upward to emphasize dominance
Distance to subject: Approximately 40–60 feet from the model
Framing: Wide shot; model positioned on the right side of the frame; crowd and arena LED screens fill the background; large negative space on the left side of the frame
Horizon: Slightly tilted upward for a grand, heroic perspective
Depth of field: Shallow — sharp focus on face and upper body; background lights soften slightly
Lens: 24–35mm full-frame equivalent; wide enough to capture the stadium without excessive distortion

Estimated Lens & Camera Settings:
Aperture: f/2.8 to f/4
Shutter speed: 1/500 to 1/1000 sec (freezes action)
ISO: 1600–3200 (indoor arena lighting)
White balance: 4500K–5000K (cool arena lighting, natural skin tones)
Focus: Continuous autofocus; focus point on model's face or upper torso
```

## Why the low-angle shot matters

The result looks cinematic because of the **low-angle perspective**:

- Model appears taller and more powerful
- The ring feels larger and more epic
- The arena seems enormous
- Spotlights converging behind the subject create a natural halo effect

This is what directors do — find a reference shot, break down *why* it works, then apply that language to your scene.

## The workflow (what you're actually learning)

You're not a designer, photographer, videographer, or director — and you don't need to be. You're using AI and references to solve a creative problem.

1. **Copy a proven prompt structure** — good starting point, not the final product
2. **Attach your reference image** — your face, body, hair
3. **Rewrite pose, wardrobe, setting** — your creative direction
4. **Find a reference shot** (Google Images, old PPV stills)
5. **Ask AI to analyze it** — *"Analyze this image. Give me the angle and camera settings."*
6. **Paste the analysis in** — adapted to your subject and scene
7. **Remove what you don't need** — text overlay, brand style blocks
8. **Generate and iterate** — if it looks right, you know which variable drove it (e.g. low angle)

> *"Good artists copy; great artists steal."* — Picasso

The steal is the **workflow and shot language**, not someone else's final image. Mix references, adapt them, and create something new.
